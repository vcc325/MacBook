## 1. Life Strategy and Priority Tiers

### The one-paragraph strategy

You are running a business, studying for a license, and rebuilding a body at the same time, on a 3:00 AM wake-up. The only way this works is if **sleep is the fixed point** everything else bends around, and if each priority gets a **protected block** instead of competing for leftover time. Your morning (3:00–7:00) is yours: training, food, and study happen before anyone can call you. Your workday (7:00–4:30) belongs to the company and has buffer blocks for the emergencies that will happen. Your evening (4:30–7:45) is short and quiet: a walk, dinner, a 10-minute reset, reading, and lights out. Saturday morning carries the Tier 3 goals in a single 90-minute block so they move forward without stealing from Tier 1. Sunday resets the week.

### Your tiers, with one adjustment

Your order is right. I made one structural change and I want to explain it.

| Tier | Priority | Daily protected time | Why it sits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 1. Sleep and recovery | 7 h 15 min in bed minimum, lights out 7:45 PM | Everything below collapses without it. On tirzepatide with a 34 lb loss behind you, under-recovery is the fastest route to losing muscle instead of fat. |
| **1** | 2. Health and nutrition | Built into meals, water, and the 30-min walk | Protects the muscle you are about to build. On appetite-suppressing medication, eating enough is a *task*, not a given. |
| **1** | 3. Construction company | 7:00 AM–4:30 PM weekdays, with buffers | Your income, your team's income, and the capital for every Tier 3 goal. |
| **1** | 4. Minnesota builder license | 60 min every weekday morning, 90 min Saturday, 30 min Sunday review (~7 h/week) | Time-boxed and front-loaded so it finishes. Once passed, its morning block hands over to business growth. |
| **1** | 5. Glute-focused recomposition | 70 min at 3:25 AM six days a week, plus the daily walk | It gets the *first* block of the day because that is the only time it reliably fits, not because it outranks the business. |
| **2** | 6. Self-care and appearance | 15 min AM, 25 min PM, 60 min Sunday | **Adjusted:** daily hygiene (floss, skin, shower, dressed intentionally) is treated as a Tier 1 health behavior and is non-negotiable even on Red days. *Appearance upgrades* (style, hair, nails, wardrobe) stay Tier 2 and live on Saturday/Sunday. |
| **2** | 7. Reading and education | 30 min every evening, 10 min minimum on bad days | Daily, small, protected. Never postponed to "when things calm down." |
| **2** | 8. Home organization | 10 min daily reset, 45 min Saturday, 60 min Sunday | Short daily maintenance so the weekend block is never a crisis. |
| **3** | 9. Amazon FBA | 45 min Saturday | Maintenance mode: keep the account healthy, no expansion until the license is done. |
| **3** | 10. Trading education | 45 min Saturday | Learning only. No live capital at risk until Tier 3 becomes Tier 2 (after licensing and the property fund hits its first milestone). |
| **3** | 11. Property / investing preparation | 30 min Sunday (fund tracking) + whatever you read in the evening reading slot | The property fund is tracked weekly so it stays real. Active renovation knowledge comes from your day job. |

**Why the hierarchy is a hierarchy:** on a Red day you drop from the bottom up. Tier 3 disappears first, then Tier 2 shrinks to its minimums (10 min reading, 10 min reset, hygiene only), and Tier 1 is protected in the order listed: sleep first, food second, business third, study fourth, training fifth. Training is the first Tier 1 item to shrink on a hard day because missing one session costs nothing, while missing sleep or a client costs a lot.

### What "measurable progress toward my goals" means each day

One checkmark per tier is a successful day:
- Tier 1: slept 7+ h, hit protein, did the business top-3, studied, and either trained or walked.
- Tier 2: read 10+ min, did the 10-min reset, did AM and PM hygiene.
- Tier 3: only on Saturday/Sunday.

## 4. The Nighttime Routine and Sleep Protection Rule

### Bedtime math

| In bed by | Lights out | Wake | Time in bed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7:30 PM** | **7:45 PM** | **3:00 AM** | **7 h 15 min** (target) |
| 8:00 PM | 8:15 PM | 3:30 AM | 7 h 15 min |
| 8:30 PM | 8:45 PM | 4:00 AM | 7 h 15 min |
| 9:00 PM | 9:15 PM | 4:30 AM | 7 h 15 min |

**The rule: wake time = lights out + 7 h 15 min. Never earlier.** A 3:00 AM alarm after a 9:00 PM lights-out is not discipline, it is a 6-hour night that costs you Monday's hip thrust, Tuesday's focus, and the muscle you are trying to protect. You are not training your body to run on less sleep. There is no adaptation to chronic short sleep, only tolerance of being worse.

### Evening shutdown (7:00–7:45 PM)

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 6:10 | Phone notifications off except crew/family emergency contacts |
| 6:30 | Reading, lamp light, no overhead lights |
| 7:00 | Quick rinse if you showered in the morning and need it; otherwise skip. Skincare (cleanse, treatment, moisturizer). Floss PM, brush. Hair prepped for the morning. |
| 7:20 | Tomorrow's clothes, gym gear, and study materials already out (done at 6:20). Glance once. Phone on its charger **outside the bedroom**. Alarm set on a separate alarm clock or the phone from across the room. |
| 7:25 | Bedroom: cool (65–68 °F), dark, quiet. Earplugs/eye mask if the household is awake. |
| 7:30 | In bed. Paper book or nothing. No screens. |
| 7:45 | Lights out. |

**Things that make a 7:45 lights-out realistic:**
- No caffeine after **12:00 PM**. At 3 AM wake-ups, noon is your "afternoon."
- Dinner finished by 6:10 PM; a small protein snack at 7:00 is fine if you are short for the day.
- No work messages after shutdown at 4:30 except true emergencies. The crew knows the plan was sent.
- Same wake time all 7 days (Sunday may float to 4:00). Consistency matters more than the number.
- Bright light in the first 30 minutes after waking and dim light after 6:30 PM. Your body clock is trained by light, not by willpower.

### Sleep protection rule (when work runs late)

Decide at **6:00 PM**, not at 3:00 AM.

| At 6:00 PM, lights out will realistically be... | Then tomorrow is a... | Tomorrow's wake | Tomorrow's morning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7:45 PM | **Green day** | 3:00 | Full plan |
| 8:15 PM | **Yellow day** | 3:30 | Workout cut to 45 min (main lift + one accessory + core), study 45 min, everything else as planned |
| 8:45 PM | **Red day** | 4:00 | Walk 30 min or a 25-min glute pump, no HIIT, no heavy lifting; study 30 min; breakfast and hygiene untouched |
| After 9:15 PM | **Red day** | 4:30 | No workout. Walk at 5 PM as usual. Study 20 min of flashcards. Protect the business day |

Two Red mornings in a row means the evening is the problem, not the morning. Audit what pushed lights-out: it is almost always work messages after 4:30 or dinner starting late.

**Never trade sleep for a workout.** A missed session costs you nothing measurable. A 6-hour night measurably lowers strength, raises hunger, and increases muscle loss in a deficit.

### Late-work evening protocol (work ends at 6:00 PM)

Construction days end late sometimes. The evening is designed so that a 6:00 finish can **still** reach a 7:45 lights-out if dinner is pre-made and you are home by about 6:20. What gets cut is the walk and part of the reading, not sleep.

**Declare it early.** If you know by the 2:30 PM buffer that work will run to 6:00, declare a late day: take the walk at lunch or as a 15-minute site walk, text yourself "late day," and tomorrow defaults to Yellow until proven Green at 6:00 PM.

| Time | Compressed evening | Normal evening it replaces |
|---|---|---|
| 6:00 | Work ends. Leave; no "one more thing." | 4:30 shutdown |
| 6:20 | Home. Dinner is reheated, not cooked (Sunday meal prep). Protein shake if appetite is gone. | 5:30 dinner |
| 6:40 | 5-minute reset: dishes in, counters clear, tomorrow's clothes, gym gear, and study materials out. Skip the weekly cleaning task. | 10-min reset + weekly task |
| 6:50 | Self-care: rinse, skincare, floss PM, brush, hair. Phone to its charger outside the bedroom. | 7:00–7:30 self-care |
| 7:10 | In bed with the book, lamp only. 10–20 pages. | 6:30–7:00 reading |
| 7:30 | Book down. | |
| **7:45–8:00** | **Lights out.** | 7:45 |
| **3:00–3:15** | **Wake.** 7 h 15 min preserved. | 3:00 |

If you are home later than 6:30, do not force it: lights out 8:15, wake 3:30, Yellow morning (45-minute session, 45-minute study). The compressed evening buys you about 25 minutes; it cannot buy you an hour.

**What the late protocol costs:** the evening walk (moved to lunch or dropped that day), 10–20 minutes of reading, and the weekly cleaning task. Nothing in Tier 1 moves.

### Frequency rule: when to change the wake time instead

Count late days per week in the tracker. Consistency of wake time beats the specific number; waking at 3:00 four days and 4:00 three days is worse for sleep quality than 3:30 every day.

| Late days (work past 5:00) per week | What to do |
|---|---|
| **0–1** | Use the late-work protocol above as an exception. Keep 3:00. |
| **2–3** | **Move the whole week to a 3:30 standard: lights out 8:15, wake 3:30, every day including weekends.** Use the 3:30 morning template below. Do not run a 3:00/3:30 split week. |
| **4+** | 4:00 standard (lights out 8:45) **and** treat the late finishes as a business problem to solve in the Friday growth block: delegation, scheduling, a hard stop the crew knows about, or an admin hire. A workday that ends at 6:00 four days a week is eating the business, not just the morning. |

### The 3:30 morning template (for 2–3 late days a week)

| Time | Block | Change from the 3:00 template |
|---|---|---|
| 3:30–3:50 | Wake, bed, face, water, dress, bite | 5 minutes shorter |
| 3:50–4:50 | **Training, 60 min** | 10 minutes shorter: core block drops to 6 minutes, last accessory cut |
| 4:50–5:00 | Cool-down, log | |
| 5:00–5:20 | Breakfast, floss | |
| 5:20–5:45 | Shower, skincare, hair, dress | 5 minutes shorter |
| 5:45–6:35 | **Study, 50 min** | 10 minutes shorter: flashcards move to lunch (10 min) |
| 6:35–6:55 | Day plan + buffer | unchanged |

Weekly study still reaches ~6.5 hours with the lunch flashcards. The program loses 10 minutes of accessories per session, which is close to irrelevant for glute growth. Sleep stays at 7 h 15 min. That trade is the correct one every time.

**Review the frequency count at the Sunday reflection.** If a month has run at the 3:30 standard and late days drop back to 0–1 a week, return to 3:00. The wake time serves the sleep, not the other way around.

## 6. Glute Program

### Where you are starting

You trained at 17 and stopped. Four years off means your strength has reset, but **muscle memory is real**: the nuclei your muscle cells added at 17 are still there, and regaining is faster than gaining the first time. Expect fast strength gains in weeks 1–6 (mostly neural), then steady loading gains. You are a "re-beginner," which is the best possible place to start a glute program.

### Equipment stages (this program is written in three stages, not three phases)

Because you have a 45 lb bar and **no plates yet**, the glute program progresses by equipment stage. Each stage is a different version of the same session. You move to the next stage the week the equipment arrives, regardless of the calendar.

| Stage | Equipment | How long you should stay here |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 0** | 45 lb bar, bands, vest, 25 lb KB, 15 lb DBs | **2–3 weeks maximum.** The empty bar is too light for hip thrusts and RDLs by week 2. Order plates in week 1. |
| **Stage 1** | + plates (and a thick bar pad) | Weeks 3–8 or longer. All three big movers are loadable. This is where most of your glute growth will come from. |
| **Stage 2** | + squat rack (and ideally a bench) | Week 6+ as budget allows. Adds back squats, barbell Bulgarian split squats, safe heavy pressing. Nice, not essential for glutes. |

**Plain statement:** with the empty bar, bands, and a vest you can build *technique* and *habit* for three weeks. You cannot build much *size* without load. Buy plates first (Section 24).

### Progression rule (all stages)

**Double progression.** Each exercise has a rep range and a set count. When **every** set hits the top of the range at the target RIR (reps in reserve), add load next session and restart at the bottom of the range.

- Barbell hip thrust, RDL, squat: +10 lb.
- Barbell lunges, step-ups, presses, rows: +5 lb.
- Dumbbell, kettlebell, vest, band moves: add reps → add the vest → add band tension → change to a harder variation.
- Miss the bottom of the range two sessions in a row → drop 5–10% and rebuild.

### Technique cues (the ones that matter most)

- **Hip thrust:** shoulder blades on the bench edge, feet so shins are vertical at the top, chin tucked, ribs down, eyes forward. Drive through heels to a full squeeze with the tailbone tucked. Pause one second. If your lower back feels it, your ribs are flaring: tuck them. Pad the bar heavily.
- **RDL:** soft knees that barely move; push the hips **back**, bar glued to the thighs, lower until a strong hamstring stretch (mid-shin for most), never past the point where your back rounds. Stand up by driving the hips forward and squeezing glutes.
- **Bulgarian split squat:** rear foot laces-down, front shin near vertical, torso leaned 10–20° forward, drive through the front heel, 2-second lowering. Weak leg first.
- **Front/Zercher squat:** elbows up (front) or bar in the crook of padded elbows (Zercher), sit between the heels, chest tall, knees over toes, to parallel or lower.
- **Step-up:** whole foot on the box, drive through the top leg, floor foot touches only lightly. Control the way down.
- **Band abduction/kickback/walks:** slow, no momentum, slight forward lean, stop before the lower back arches.

## 7. Deep-Core Program

### Strengthening abs vs. seeing abs

Direct core training makes the rectus abdominis, obliques, and transverse abdominis stronger and slightly thicker, which makes them **more visible at any body fat** and gives you the "tight" look and better bracing for hip thrusts and RDLs. Visible abs and a smaller-looking waist come from body fat coming down, which is nutrition and the medication, plus posture and deep-core control. No exercise burns fat off your midsection. I will not pretend otherwise.

Your waist can *look* smaller through three real mechanisms: fat loss, better transverse abdominis tone (the "corset" muscle, trained by bracing and anti-movement work), and bigger glutes and shoulders changing the ratio. All three are in this plan.

### Principles

- Four exposures a week (Mon, Thu, Fri, plus Wednesday Pilates), 10 minutes each.
- Anti-extension, anti-rotation, lower-ab control, and bracing get most of the volume. Flexion work (crunch-type) is limited to one weighted movement per week for hypertrophy. **No endless crunches.**
- The ab wheel is the anti-extension centerpiece and progresses over months, not weeks.

### Core A — Monday (10 min): anti-extension + bracing

| Movement | Weeks 1–4 | Weeks 5–8 | Weeks 9–12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **90/90 breathing** (feet on a box, exhale fully, feel the lower ribs drop) | 2 × 5 breaths | 1 × 5 | 1 × 5 |
| **Ab wheel rollout** | Kneeling, partial range (roll to where the back wants to arch, stop), 3 × 6–8 | Kneeling, full range to the floor, 3 × 6–10 | Kneeling full, 3 × 10–12, 3-s return; or kneeling from an elevated box for a harder angle |
| **Dead bug with band** (band anchored behind the head, held taut with straight arms) | 3 × 8/side | 3 × 10/side | 3 × 10/side, 3-s hold |
| **Hollow hold** (bent knees → straight legs) | 3 × 15–20 s | 3 × 25–30 s | 3 × 30–40 s |

### Core B — Thursday (10 min): anti-rotation + lower-ab control + one hypertrophy movement

| Movement | Weeks 1–4 | Weeks 5–8 | Weeks 9–12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Band Pallof press** (band anchored at chest height, press out, resist rotation) | 3 × 10/side | 3 × 12/side | 3 × 12/side, 2-s hold at full extension, heavier band |
| **Lying leg lowering** (heels toward floor, back pressed flat, stop before it arches) | 3 × 8 bent-knee | 3 × 10 straight-leg | 3 × 12 straight-leg + hip lift at the top |
| **Bird dog** | 2 × 8/side, 2-s hold | 2 × 8/side, 3-s hold | 2 × 10/side with a band around the hands and feet |
| **Side plank** | 2 × 20–30 s/side | 2 × 30–40 s/side | 2 × 40 s/side, top leg lifted |
| **Kneeling band crunch** (band anchored high, curl ribs toward hips) — the one hypertrophy movement | 2 × 12 | 3 × 12 | 3 × 12–15, heavier band |

### Core C — Friday (10 min): Peloton Crush Your Core

Pick 10-minute "core strength" or "abs and obliques" classes. Avoid classes built on hundreds of crunches. This class **replaces** a core block; it is never stacked on Core A or B.

### Wednesday — reformer / mat Pilates (deep core inside the 40-min session)

Your home reformer session should spend 10–15 of its 40 minutes on footwork, feet-in-straps lower-ab series, plank series on the carriage, and side-lying leg work. This is your transverse abdominis and pelvic-control dose for the week.

### Ab wheel progression (the long game)

1. Kneeling partial (stop at the first hint of back arch) → 2. Kneeling full to the floor → 3. Kneeling full with 3-s return → 4. Kneeling from an elevated box → 5. Standing partial against a wall (the wheel stops at the wall; walk the wall out a foot at a time) → 6. Standing full. Steps 1–3 are this 12 weeks; 4–5 are months 4–9; 6 is a year or more. Never let the lower back arch; if it does, you have gone too far.

## 9. Nutrition Framework (on tirzepatide)

### The situation, stated plainly

You lost ~34 lb without training. Studies of GLP-1 and GIP/GLP-1 medications suggest that without resistance training and adequate protein, a meaningful share of weight lost (often estimated at 25–40%) can be lean mass. Some of that is water and organ tissue rather than muscle, but some is muscle. From this point on, the training and the protein are what decide whether the next pounds come from fat or from the glutes you want.

The medication is already creating a calorie deficit by suppressing appetite. **Your job is no longer to eat less. It is to eat enough, especially protein, so the deficit the medication creates is filled with muscle-protecting food.** That is a different mindset from your last 34 lb.

### Targets (estimates, corrected by your weekly data)

| Target | Daily | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Estimated maintenance** | ~2,350–2,550 kcal | Mifflin-St Jeor at your stats, moderately active. ±10–15% error |
| **Intake target** | **2,000–2,200 kcal**. Start at 2,100 | A small deficit (10–15%). The medication will push you under this if you let it. Treat the number as a *floor to reach*, not a ceiling |
| **Hard floor** | **1,800 kcal** | Below this for more than a few days in a row and you are losing muscle regardless of training. Tell your clinician if you cannot reach it |
| **Protein** | **150–165 g** (0.9–1.0 g per lb) | The single most important number. Spread across 4–5 feedings |
| **Fat** | **60–70 g**, never below 50 g | Hormones, satiety, vitamin absorption |
| **Carbohydrates** | **200–240 g** | Fuels the 3:25 AM sessions, the runs, and the HIIT; refills glycogen so glutes grow |
| **Fiber** | **25–30 g**, built up gradually | Satiety, gut health. Tirzepatide slows gastric emptying; too much fiber too fast causes bloating and nausea, so add 5 g a week |
| **Water** | **2.5–3 L (85–100 oz)** + electrolytes on training, HIIT, and heated days | Dehydration and medication-related nausea feed each other |
| **Micronutrients** | Iron, calcium, vitamin D, B12, magnesium, potassium, omega-3 | Reduced total intake means reduced micronutrient intake. Ask your clinician about labs and a basic multivitamin, vitamin D, and omega-3 |

Checksum: 160 g protein (640) + 65 g fat (585) + 220 g carbs (880) = 2,105 kcal.

### Meal structure that works with appetite suppression

Four to five **smaller** feedings, each with 30–40 g protein. Large meals sit badly on tirzepatide; small frequent ones get the protein in.

| Feeding | Time | Protein | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-workout bite | 3:15 AM | 0–10 g | 100–150 kcal carbs; whey in water if solid food is unappealing |
| **Breakfast** | 4:45 AM | 40 g | Greek yogurt + whey + berries + oats; or 3 eggs + egg whites + toast + fruit |
| **Lunch** | 12:00 PM | 35–40 g | Chicken, lean beef, fish, tofu + rice/potato + vegetables + olive oil |
| **Afternoon** | 2:30–3:00 PM (Buffer 2) | 25–30 g | Protein shake, cottage cheese + fruit, jerky + fruit, or a protein bar |
| **Dinner** | 5:30 PM | 40–45 g | Lean protein + carb + two vegetables |
| Optional | 7:00 PM | 15–20 g | Casein or cottage cheese if the daily total is short |

**Liquid protein is your friend.** A whey or whey-casein shake (25–40 g) takes 2 minutes to drink and does not trigger fullness the way solid food does. Two shakes a day is fine on this medication.

**Carb timing:** push carbs toward the pre-workout bite, breakfast, and dinner (which is the meal before tomorrow's 3:25 AM session). Keep lunch moderate so you are not sluggish on site.

**Pre-workout at 3:15:** small, carb-based, low fat and fiber. **Post-workout at 4:45:** the breakfast. There is no 30-minute window; the meal matters, the stopwatch does not.

**Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g daily** is well supported for strength and lean mass, inexpensive, and safe for healthy adults. Expect 1–3 lb of water weight in weeks 1–2. Mention it to your clinician alongside everything else in Section 25.

### Rules for recognizing under-eating

You are under-eating if **two or more** of these are true for a week:

- Weekly average weight dropping faster than **1% per week** (more than ~1.7 lb/week at your weight) for two or more weeks.
- Hip thrust or RDL reps/loads going **down** at the same RIR, two sessions in a row.
- Hip/glute measurement dropping while waist drops. (Waist down, hips stable or up = winning. Both down fast = losing muscle.)
- Waking tired after 7+ hours in bed for 4+ nights; resting heart rate up 5+ bpm.
- Cold hands and feet, hair shedding, brittle nails, irregular or missed menstrual cycle.
- Constant thoughts about food despite low appetite; or so little appetite you "forget" meals until evening.
- Dizziness standing up; headaches; poor concentration in the 5:35 study block.
- Fewer than 1,800 kcal or fewer than 130 g protein on three or more days in a week.

**If two or more are true:** raise intake 150–200 kcal/day for two weeks (mostly carbs and a shake), and tell your clinician. If it persists at the higher intake, the conversation about dose and goals in Section 25 needs to happen now, not later.

### Adjustment rules (every 3–4 weeks, using weekly averages)

| Weight trend | Lifts | Waist/hips | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| −0.3 to −0.8 lb/week | Up or stable | Waist down, hips stable | **Perfect. Change nothing.** |
| −0.8 to −1.7 lb/week | Up or stable | Waist down, hips stable | Acceptable on medication; watch for under-eating signs. Consider +100 kcal |
| Faster than −1.7 lb/week | Any | Any | **+150–200 kcal/day** and talk to your clinician |
| Stable | Up | Waist down, hips stable or up | Recomposition working. Change nothing |
| Stable 3+ weeks | Stalling | No change | Check protein and sleep first. Then, and only then, −100 kcal |
| Dropping | Dropping | Hips dropping | Under-eating. +200 kcal immediately and call your clinician |

### How to know you are losing fat, not muscle

Over any 4-week block, you are on track if **two or more** of these hold: waist down (even 0.5 in) with hips stable or up; hip thrust and RDL loads or reps up; progress photos showing more glute shape and more arm/midsection definition; body weight stable or slowly down. You do not need to reach a particular number on the scale to get the look you described.

### Arms, specifically

Your arms will look leaner as body fat comes down. The two upper-body sessions keep the muscle underneath so the arm looks firm rather than soft. You are not going to "bulk" your arms with 4 direct sets a week at 15 lb; that is not how female arm hypertrophy works at this volume. Keep the upper days, skip the arm specialization, and let the deficit do the rest.

## 17. Ice Bath Plan

### Start date and placement

- **Start in week 4** (Sunday, November 8), once the routine has held for three weeks. It is a reward for consistency, not a tool for results.
- **Once a week, Sunday 1:00–1:20 PM.** Sunday is the only day with no resistance training, which is exactly where cold immersion belongs.

### Does timing matter? Yes.

Cold water immersion in the hours after resistance training **blunts the muscle-building signal** from that session (reduced satellite cell activity, reduced protein synthesis response, and in longer studies smaller strength and size gains). The effect is real enough that no ice bath should happen within ~6 hours of a lifting session, and preferably not on a lifting day at all. Sunday afternoon is 24+ hours from Saturday's lift and 14+ hours before Monday's. That is the right slot.

### What it is and is not

- It **is**: an enjoyable, short recovery ritual; a mild boost to perceived recovery and soreness; a mental-toughness habit you want.
- It **is not**: a fat-loss tool (the calorie effect is trivial), a muscle-growth tool (the opposite, if mistimed), or necessary for anything in this plan. If you stop enjoying it, stop.

### Protocol

| Weeks | Water temperature | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 4–6 | 55–60 °F (13–15 °C) | 2–3 min |
| 7–9 | 50–55 °F (10–13 °C) | 3–5 min |
| 10+ | 50–55 °F | 5–8 min max |

- Never alone in the house the first few times. Enter slowly, breathe slowly and out long; never hold your breath or hyperventilate. Hands out of the water if needed.
- Get out at shivering that you cannot control, numbness in hands/feet, or any dizziness.
- Rewarm by dressing warm and moving; no hot shower for 15–20 minutes.
- Skip it when you are sick, after a poor night, on an empty stomach, or if your clinician has any cardiovascular concern. Mention it in the Section 25 conversation.

## 25. What to Discuss With Your Prescribing Clinician

Bring this list to your next appointment. These are the things a clinician should weigh in on; I am not able to.

**About the medication and your goals**
1. You have lost ~34 lb without training, and your goal has changed: you want body *composition* change, not continued weight loss at any cost. Ask how they think about dose (staying at 10 mg vs. titrating up, holding, or eventually tapering) when the patient's goal is muscle preservation and gain.
