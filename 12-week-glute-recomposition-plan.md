## 1. Physique Strategy and Training Priorities

### The big picture

Your goal has two separate drivers that need two separate tools:

| What you want | What actually produces it | What does *not* produce it |
|---|---|---|
| Bigger, rounder, higher glutes | Progressive overload on hip thrusts, RDLs, and loaded unilateral work, 2–3 times per week, with enough food and sleep to recover | Endless high-rep bodyweight sets, "booty burn" classes, cardio |
| Visible abs, defined triceps, tighter waist | Building the muscle (direct ab and triceps work) **and** gradually lowering body fat through a modest calorie deficit | Ab exercises alone; sweating; heated classes |
| Lean, athletic appearance | Keeping lower-body muscle while body fat trends down slowly (recomposition) | Aggressive dieting, which costs you the glute muscle you are trying to build |

### Why recomposition, not a hard cut

At 21, with a body weight of 171.9 lb and a background that is probably beginner-to-intermediate, you are in the single best position for **recomposition**: gaining muscle while losing fat at the same time. Newer lifters, lifters returning after a break, and lifters with body fat above the very-lean range can do this reliably. The trade is that each process is slower than it would be if you did only one. For your priorities (glutes first, leanness second) that is the right trade: a steep deficit would make your glute training stall by week 4.

So the program uses a **small deficit (roughly 10–12% below estimated maintenance)**, high protein, and glute training that is heavy enough to force adaptation. You will not be optimizing for calorie burn. Most of your cardio is low-impact and deliberately short.

### Training priorities in order

1. **Glutes** — 3 exposures per week: two heavy sessions (Monday, Thursday) and one lighter pump/metabolic session (Saturday). ~26 weekly hard sets for glutes + hamstrings in Phase 1, rising to ~32–34 in Phase 3.
2. **Abs/core** — 3 structured sessions (attached to Monday, Thursday, Friday) plus the core component of Wednesday heated Pilates. Short and progressive, not a 30-minute burnout.
3. **Triceps** — 2 upper-body days (Tuesday, Friday) with 10–12 direct triceps sets per week in Phase 1, rising to 14–16. Arms with Tunde slots in as a *replacement* for part of a session, never stacked on top.
4. **Body composition** — handled by nutrition and consistency, not by adding more cardio.
5. **Conditioning / mobility** — one impact HIIT or HYROX-style session (Saturday), one low-impact ride (Tuesday), one heated Pilates (Wednesday), optional gentle Pilates or a walk on Sunday.

### Hard days vs. training days

You train 6 days and rest 1. Only **three** of those days are hard: Monday, Thursday, and Saturday. Tuesday and Friday are moderate upper-body days. Wednesday is a moderate mobility/control day. Sunday is rest or a walk. If you ever feel the plan is "too easy" in week 1, that is intentional. Phase 1 establishes technique and a baseline so the loading in Phases 2 and 3 can actually go up.

## 3. The Workouts

**Notation:** Sets × reps · rest · target RIR (reps in reserve — how many clean reps you could still do when you stop). RIR 2 means stop two reps short of failure. "Phase 1 / Phase 2 / Phase 3" shows how sets and reps change across the 12 weeks.

**Warm-up for every strength day (6–8 min):** 2 min easy jump rope or bike → 10 bodyweight glute bridges, 10 bodyweight squats, 10 hip hinges with the empty bar, 5 inchworms. Then 1–2 ramp-up sets of the first exercise with light weight.

**Technique cues**

- **Hip thrust:** shoulder blades on the bench edge; feet so shins are vertical at the top; chin tucked, ribs down, eyes forward not up; drive through heels; finish with a full posterior pelvic tilt (squeeze glutes, tuck tailbone) and pause 1 s at the top; lower with control, do not let the bar bounce. If your lower back is doing the work, your ribs are flaring: tuck them. Pad the bar.
- **Front/Zercher squat:** sit between the heels, keep the chest tall, let knees travel over toes, depth to parallel or lower. Zercher: bar in the crooks of the elbows, hands clasped, pad the bar.
- **Bulgarian split squat:** rear foot laces-down on bench; front shin roughly vertical to parallel; lean torso 10–20° forward to bias glutes; drive through the front heel; control the descent (2 s). Do the weaker leg first.
- **Single-leg hip thrust:** pelvis stays level, no rotation; the resting leg can hover or be bent at 90°.
- **Side-lying abduction:** top leg slightly behind the body and toes pointed down toward the floor; lift from the hip, not by rolling the torso back.
- **Reverse crunch:** curl the pelvis off the floor using the abs, not momentum from the legs; lower slowly.
- **Dead bug:** low back pressed flat into the floor the entire time; move slowly; exhale as the limbs extend.

**Technique cues**

- **Floor press:** lying on the floor, grip about shoulder width (hands just inside shoulders), elbows tucked at ~45°, lower until the upper arms touch the floor, pause 1 s, press. Set up by rolling the bar over you from a plate-stacked position, or deadlift it to your hips and lie back.
- **Overhead press:** squeeze glutes and brace abs so the ribs don't flare; bar path is straight up, head moves back then "through" at the top.
- **Close-grip push-up:** hands directly under shoulders, elbows tracking back, body rigid from head to heels.
- **Overhead extension:** elbows pointing forward and staying close to the head; lower until a full triceps stretch; drive the weight up by extending the elbows, not by shrugging.
- **Kickback:** torso parallel to the floor, upper arm locked at your side and parallel to the floor, only the forearm moves. Squeeze at lockout.

**Technique cues**

- **RDL:** soft knees that stay in roughly the same position; push the hips **back** rather than bending forward; bar stays touching the thighs; lower until you feel a strong hamstring stretch (usually mid-shin), never past the point where your lower back rounds; stand up by driving hips forward and squeezing glutes. Double-overhand grip until grip becomes the limiter, then mixed grip or straps.
- **Reverse lunge:** step back far enough that the front shin stays near vertical; push through the front heel to return; slight forward lean for glutes.
- **Step-up:** box at knee height or slightly above; place the whole foot on the box; drive through the top foot and *do not* push off the floor foot (just touch the toe lightly). Control the descent.
- **Single-leg RDL:** hinge at the hip with the back leg reaching straight behind, hips square to the floor; holding the KB in the hand opposite the standing leg gives a glute med bias.
- **Kickback:** squeeze the glute to extend the hip, stop before the lower back arches.
- **Leg raise:** keep the lower back pressed down; stop lowering when the back wants to arch.
- **Suitcase carry:** stand perfectly tall, do not let the weight pull you sideways; breathe.

## 4. 12-Week Glute Progression Plan

### The progression rule (applies to every main lift)

**Double progression.** Each exercise has a rep range (e.g., 8–10) and a set count. Work with a load where the *first* set hits the top of the range at the target RIR. When **all** sets hit the top of the range at or above target RIR, add load next session and start over at the bottom of the range.

- Barbell lifts: add **5 lb** (2.5 per side) for upper body and unilateral moves, **10 lb** for hip thrusts, RDLs, and squats.
- Dumbbell/kettlebell/vest moves: add reps first, then add the vest, then switch to a harder variation (Section 4.3).
- If you miss the bottom of the rep range on two consecutive sessions, drop the load 5–10% and rebuild.

### 4.1 Phase-by-phase

**Phase 1 — Weeks 1–4: Technique, baseline, tolerable volume**

- RIR 3 on the main barbell lifts in weeks 1–2, RIR 2–3 in weeks 3–4.
- Week 1 is for finding working weights. Start lighter than you think. A hip thrust set of 12 with RIR 3 should feel like you could do 15.
- Weekly glute + hamstring hard sets: ~26 (Mon 13, Thu 14, Sat ~8 light sets counted as half).
- Film your hip thrust and RDL from the side in weeks 1 and 4.
- **What should change by week 4:** your hip thrust and RDL loads should have gone up 2–4 times via double progression; your form should be automatic.

**Phase 2 — Weeks 5–8: Load and volume go up, fatigue is managed**

- Add a 4th set to hip thrust, RDL, floor press, and rows. Rep ranges shift down slightly (8–10 instead of 10–12) so load can go up.
- RIR 2 on main lifts.
- Weekly glute + hamstring hard sets: ~30.
- **Week 8 is a conditional light week.** If you have ≥2 of these fatigue flags in week 7 (sleep worse than usual for 4+ nights, resting heart rate up 5+ bpm, main lifts went down or felt much heavier at the same load, joint aches, motivation gone), then in week 8 cut every exercise to 2 sets, keep the same loads, skip Saturday conditioning, and keep Pilates gentle. If you have fewer than 2 flags, train week 8 as normal.

**Phase 3 — Weeks 9–12: Heavier loading, assess, deload if needed**

- Hip thrust and RDL move to 4 × 6–8 (RIR 1–2) plus one back-off set of 12–15 on the hip thrust. This is where you touch your heaviest weights of the block.
- Unilateral work is loaded as heavy as your equipment allows (Section 4.3).
- Weekly glute + hamstring hard sets: ~32–34.
- **Week 12:** on Monday, work up to a 6-rep hip thrust at RIR 1 and record it; on Thursday, the same for RDL. These are your block benchmarks. After week 12, take a full deload week (50% sets, 80% loads, no HIIT) before starting the next block.

### 4.2 Expected barbell progression (illustrative, not a promise)

Assuming you start as a beginner-to-early-intermediate and progress normally:

| Lift | Week 1 (3 × 10–12, RIR 3) | Week 4 | Week 8 (4 × 8–10) | Week 12 (4 × 6–8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barbell hip thrust | 65–95 lb | 95–125 lb | 135–165 lb | 165–205 lb |
| Barbell RDL | 65–85 lb | 85–105 lb | 105–135 lb | 125–155 lb |
| Front/Zercher squat | 45–65 lb | 65–85 lb | 85–105 lb | 95–125 lb |

If your current numbers are already above the week-1 column, start where you actually are. The right of the table is only a guide to make sure your **plates are sufficient**: you need at least four 45s or the equivalent to finish Phase 3 on hip thrusts.

### 4.3 When your dumbbells and kettlebell run out

Honest assessment of your equipment:

| Movement | Your current max load | When it becomes insufficient | Progression path |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulgarian split squat | 2 × 15 lb DBs (30 lb) or 24 lb KB, + vest | Likely by **week 4–6** (you will hit 12 reps at RIR 2 with everything you own) | 1. Add vest → 2. Slow 3 s eccentric + 1 s pause at the bottom → 3. Deficit (front foot on a plate) → 4. **Barbell** front-rack or Zercher BSS, or buy heavier DBs/KB |
| Reverse lunge | Same | **Week 4–6** | Same path; barbell front-rack reverse lunge is excellent and needs no rack |
| Step-up | Same | **Week 5–7** | Higher box → vest → barbell front rack |
| Single-leg RDL | 24 lb KB + vest | Week 8+ (balance limits this anyway) | Keep it as a higher-rep accessory; the barbell RDL carries the heavy load |
| Overhead triceps extension | 24 lb KB two-handed | Week 8–10 | Single-arm 15 lb DB extension (harder per arm) → barbell French press → heavier DB |
| KB row | 24 lb | Week 3–4 | Barbell row carries the load; convert KB row to a slow-tempo accessory or a barbell "meadows" row |
| Abduction/kickback/frog pump | Bodyweight/vest/DB on thigh | Not a problem — these respond fine to high reps | Add reps, then vest, then slow tempo |

**The three big movers (hip thrust, RDL, squat variation) are barbell lifts, so your glute program is not capped by your dumbbells.** The dumbbells and KB limit the *unilateral* leg work and some upper-body work. You have three choices when that limit arrives, roughly in weeks 4–6:

1. **Free: switch to barbell variants.** Front-rack reverse lunges, Zercher Bulgarian split squats, and barbell step-ups all work without a rack. These are the recommended default.
2. **Cheap ($15–30): a set of loop/mini resistance bands.** Not for loading the big lifts, but bands make abduction, kickbacks, Pallof presses, and banded hip thrusts far more effective. This is the single best small purchase for glute medius work.
3. **Moderate ($60–150): one heavier kettlebell (35 lb and/or 53 lb) or a pair of adjustable dumbbells.** This extends your unilateral loading by 2–3 months and makes goblet squats, KB swings, and single-arm rows worthwhile again.

What I will **not** tell you is that 30 reps with 15 lb dumbbells gives the same result as 10 reps with a weight that is actually heavy for you. Past ~30 reps per set the stimulus for growth drops off sharply and the limiting factor becomes burning and boredom rather than muscle tension. When a set of 15 at RIR 2 becomes easy and you cannot add load, change the exercise rather than adding more reps.

### 4.4 Why the balance of work looks the way it does

- **Gluteus maximus** (size and projection): hip thrusts, glute bridges, RDLs, squats, lunges, step-ups. The hip thrust loads the glute hardest at full hip extension; the RDL and squat load it in the stretched position. You need both.
- **Gluteus medius / upper glute** ("shelf" and width): abduction, single-leg stance work (BSS, step-ups, single-leg RDL), kickbacks with slight abduction, side-lying work. This is why unilateral work stays in the program even when it is lightly loaded.
- **Hamstrings:** RDLs and single-leg RDLs. Enough for balance; not so much that they steal recovery from glutes.
- **Quads:** squat variation, BSS, reverse lunge, step-ups. Enough for athletic legs and knee health; they are not a priority so there is no leg extension, no high-volume quad work.

### Weekly dose

### Movement roles

| Movement | Why it's here | Progression |
|---|---|---|
| **Close-grip floor press** | Heaviest triceps loading you can do without a rack; trains the long and lateral heads under load | +5 lb when 4 × 10 at RIR 2 |
| **Overhead extension (KB or DB)** | Long-head emphasis in the stretched position — the head that gives the arm its "defined" look from behind | Reps → single-arm 15 lb → barbell French press |
| **Skull crusher** | Mid-range, all heads, moderate load | +5 lb barbell or move from DBs to the bar |
| **Kickback** | Peak contraction at lockout; light load is fine | Strict form, 2 s squeeze, then 3 sets |
| **Close-grip push-up / bench dip** | Bodyweight compound; easy to progress with the vest | Vest → feet elevated → plate on lap (dips) |

### Arms with Tunde

Those classes typically contain 100–200+ reps of light shoulder, biceps, and triceps work. They are good for muscular endurance, "pump," and enjoyment, and they contain far more shoulder volume than you need. Use them this way:

- **Do not** stack a 20-minute Arms with Tunde class on top of a full Upper A or Upper B session. That is 60–70 minutes of arm work and the only thing it adds is fatigue.
- **Do** use a 10–15 minute class as a *replacement* for B5 + B6 on Tuesday (kickbacks + lateral raises) every other week, or as a replacement for D5 + D6 on Friday. Use your 5–10 lb dumbbells, not the 15s, and follow her tempo.
- If you do a class, count the triceps portion as ~3 direct sets for that day's tally.
- Skip classes that are 30+ minutes while on this program; they will leave you too fatigued for Thursday's RDLs (your grip and shoulders need to be fresh for heavy hinging).

### Recovery

Elbow tendons adapt more slowly than muscle. If you feel elbow pain (not muscle burn) during extensions or skull crushers, swap that session's extensions for close-grip push-ups and reduce direct sets by 30% for two weeks.

## 6. Progressive Ab/Core Plan

### Strengthening abs vs. seeing abs

These are two different projects. **Ab training** makes the rectus abdominis and obliques thicker and stronger, which makes them more visible *at any given body fat*. **Body-fat reduction** is what removes the layer over them, and it happens across the whole body in an order set by genetics. There is no exercise that burns fat specifically from your midsection, and no exercise that "shrinks your waist." A waist measurement goes down because body fat goes down (nutrition) and because posture and core control improve (training). I will not tell you otherwise.

That said, direct ab training is still worth doing for you: it improves bracing for hip thrusts and RDLs, protects your lower back, and makes your abs pop more clearly as body fat comes down.

### Structure

| Session | When | Type | Exercises |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core A** | Mon, after Lower A | Flexion + anti-extension | Reverse crunch, dead bug, plank |
| **Core B** | Thu, after Lower B | Flexion + anti-rotation + anti-lateral flexion | Lying leg raise, anti-rotation press-out, suitcase carry |
| **Core C** | Fri, after Upper B | Peloton Crush Your Core | 10–15 min class |
| **Pilates** | Wed | Control, deep core, breathing | As programmed by the instructor |

Each core block takes 8–10 minutes. **Four exposures per week** total.

### Progression

| Weeks | Reverse crunch | Leg raise | Dead bug | Plank | Anti-rotation | Carry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | 3 × 10–12 | 3 × 10 bent-knee | 3 × 8/side | 3 × 30 s | 3 × 10/side | 2 × 30 m |
| 5–8 | 3 × 12–15 | 3 × 12 straight-leg | 3 × 10/side with DB | 3 × 40 s | 3 × 12/side | 3 × 30 m |
| 9–12 | 3 × 15, 3 s lower, then add a DB between the feet | 3 × 12–15 with hip lift at top | 3 × 10/side with 3 s hold | 3 × 45 s in vest | 3 × 12 with 2 s hold, heavier DB | 3 × 40 m with vest |

Weighted options once bodyweight is easy: hold a plate on the chest for reverse crunches, a DB between the feet for leg raises, a KB on the back for planks (have someone place it), the KB for "weighted dead bug" (held over the chest).

### Crush Your Core — how to use it

- A 10-minute class is roughly 4–6 hard sets of core work. It **replaces** a core block; it is never added on top of Core A or Core B.
- Default: Friday after Upper B. Choose "core strength" or "abs and obliques" classes; avoid the ones built around high-rep flexion (hundreds of crunches), which add fatigue and little else.
- If you love the program and want to do it more than once a week, do it on Friday **and** Sunday (low intensity), and drop the plank/carry portion from Core A or Core B that week.
- If a class leaves your lower back sore rather than your abs, you are flexing from the spine instead of the hips, or arching during leg lowering. Pick easier variations.

## 7. Heated Pilates Integration

### How much and when

- **Start with 1 class per week on Wednesday.** It sits 48 hours after Monday's heavy session (soreness peaks days 1–2, so by Wednesday you are through the worst) and 24 hours before Thursday's heavy session.
- **Optional second class on Sunday** from week 3 onward, provided Sunday's class is low intensity or non-heated. If a Sunday class leaves you sore, it is not a recovery session and it stays off the schedule.
- Never on Monday, Thursday, or Saturday.

### Is a class training load or recovery?

Rate every class on the 1–10 effort scale afterwards:

| Class felt like | Count it as | Adjust |
|---|---|---|
| 3–5/10: breathing easy, flexibility and control work, minimal shaking | Active recovery / mobility | Nothing |
| 6–7/10: sustained glute/leg burning, side-lying series, lots of bridging and planking | A **light glute + core session** | Saturday Glute C drops to 2 sets per exercise that week; skip Core A's plank |
| 8+/10: legs shaking, many lunges/squats/jump-free "cardio" sets, you are wrecked | A hard session (and the wrong class for this program) | Pick a different class/instructor next week. If unavoidable, drop Saturday's Glute C entirely and keep conditioning easy |

Heated Pilates is neither useless cardio nor a free recovery day. Most reformer/mat classes at 95–105 °F land around 5–6/10 for someone your age and conditioning: they raise heart rate, build hip and spine mobility, and train the deep core and glute medius in ways the barbell work does not. That is why it is in the plan.

### Heat, hydration, electrolytes

- **Before:** 16–20 oz of water in the 1–2 hours before class, with ~500 mg sodium (an electrolyte packet or a salty snack). Do not arrive fasted; eat a small carb + protein snack 60–90 minutes before.
- **During:** sip 8–16 oz across the class.
- **After:** weigh yourself before and after one class (dry off first). Each pound lost is roughly 16–20 oz of fluid to replace over the next 2–3 hours, ideally with electrolytes. If you lose more than 2% of body weight (about 3.5 lb for you) in a class, you are under-drinking for the heat.
- **Extra on Wednesdays:** add 500–1000 mg sodium to your day. Your training sweat plus the heated class puts you above the typical sodium needs.
- **Stop and cool down immediately** if you get dizzy, nauseous, get a headache, feel unusually weak or confused, stop sweating while still hot, or have heart palpitations. Leave the room, lie down with your feet up, sip cool fluids. Do not return to class that day. If symptoms are severe or do not resolve in 20–30 minutes, seek medical attention.
- Heated classes are not advised if you are unwell, have a fever, are hungover, or have had very poor sleep: do a non-heated class or a walk.

### What heated Pilates will and won't do

Will: hip and thoracic mobility, pelvic control, glute med and deep core endurance, better bracing for your lifts, enjoyable sweat. Won't: build significant glute size (not enough load), burn meaningful fat (sweat is water, not fat), replace a strength session.

## 8. HIIT, HYROX, Cycling, and Rowing Integration

### The dose

| Type | Where | Weekly dose | Intensity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Impact / sprint HIIT (HYROX-style)** | Saturday after Glute C | 1 × 20–25 min | Hard, but short |
| **Low-impact aerobic ride** | Tuesday after Upper A | 1 × 20–30 min | Easy to moderate (you can speak in full sentences) |
| **Optional easy cardio** | Friday or Sunday | 15–45 min walk or recovery ride | Easy |

That is it. One hard conditioning session per week is enough to improve your fitness meaningfully while you are also doing three glute sessions and two upper sessions. Adding a second HIIT session would be the first thing to cut into your hip thrust and RDL progress, because jumping, sprinting, and rowing all load the glutes, hamstrings, and calves.

### Saturday conditioning — three rotating options

**Option 1 — HYROX-style circuit (home, 22 min):** 4 rounds, 90 s rest between rounds, as hard as sustainable:
- 400 m run (or 3 min hard bike if no run route), 15 KB swings (24 lb), 10 burpee broad jumps (or 20 jump rope double-unders / 60 single-unders), 10 walking lunges each leg in vest, 15 KB sumo high-pulls or 10 KB goblet squats.
- Scale by rounds (start with 3) not by pace.

**Option 2 — Peloton HYROX Training Program session:** choose the program's **bike** or **strength/conditioning** class for that week. Skip or modify sled pushes, sled pulls, and wall balls (no equipment) by substituting KB swings, vest walking lunges, or jump rope intervals. Do **not** try to follow the full program's 3–4 classes per week on top of this plan; take one class per week and treat the rest of the program as a library.

**Option 3 — Peloton HIIT ride or Tabata ride (20–30 min):** low-impact way to get the same heart-rate stimulus. Use this option in any week where your legs are still sore on Saturday morning from Thursday, or when a heated Pilates class was unusually hard.

**Rowing:** if you have a rower, a 20-minute interval row (e.g., 8 × 1 min hard / 1 min easy) is an excellent Option 3 substitute. Rowing loads the glutes, hamstrings, and back heavily, so it belongs on Saturday, never Wednesday or Sunday.

### Tuesday low-impact ride

Power Zone Endurance, Low Impact, or a 20–30 minute scenic/music ride at a conversational effort. Purpose: aerobic base, blood flow to Monday's trained legs, and enjoyment. If you are short on time, this is the first session to cut.

### Which classes to avoid stacking

| Don't stack | Why |
|---|---|
| Arms with Tunde (20+ min) **after** a full Upper A/B | Redundant shoulder volume, elbows get cranky |
| Crush Your Core **and** Core A or Core B on the same day | Doubling core work gives soreness, not more abs |
| Any HIIT or HYROX class on Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, or Sunday | Mon/Thu: competes with heavy glute work. Wed/Sun: those are your recovery buffers |
| Two heated Pilates classes in a row (Wed + Thu) | Heat stress plus a heavy RDL day is a recipe for a flat session |
| Peloton "Bootcamp" (bike + floor) classes in general | They include lots of lunges/squats/jumping; count as a leg day. If you want one, it replaces Saturday's Glute C + conditioning entirely |

### When low-impact is the better choice

Choose a ride or walk instead of HIIT whenever: your quads or glutes are still sore on Saturday; your sleep this week averaged under 7 hours; your hip thrust went down on Monday; you are in week 8 light week; or you are feeling run-down. Low-impact cardio improves recovery and aerobic fitness. HIIT on a tired body improves neither; it only adds fatigue.

## 9. Nutrition Targets and Meal-Planning Framework

### Estimated maintenance (estimates, not measurements)

Using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation at 171.9 lb (78 kg), 5'4" (163 cm), age 21:

- **Basal metabolic rate:** ~1,530 kcal/day
- **Estimated maintenance** at your activity level (6 training days plus daily life): **~2,350–2,550 kcal/day**

Individual error on these equations is ±10–15%. Your real maintenance is whatever intake holds your weight stable over 2–3 weeks. We start with the estimate and correct it with data.

### Recommended approach: recomposition with a modest deficit

| Target | Daily amount | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Calories** | **~2,150–2,250 kcal** (start at 2,200) | Roughly 10–12% below estimated maintenance. Enough of a deficit to lose fat slowly (0.3–0.7 lb per week), small enough that glute training keeps progressing. |
| **Protein** | **150–160 g** (~0.9 g per lb) | High protein protects muscle in a deficit, supports growth, and keeps you full. 160 g is plenty; more does not help. |
| **Fat** | **60–70 g** (~0.35–0.4 g per lb) | Enough for hormone health and satiety. Do not go below 55 g. |
| **Carbohydrates** | **~220–240 g** | Fills the remaining calories. Carbs drive your hard training sessions and refill muscle glycogen; low-carb is the wrong tool for glute growth. |
| **Fiber** | **28–32 g** | ~14 g per 1,000 kcal. From vegetables, fruit, oats, beans, whole grains. |

Checksum: 155 g protein (620 kcal) + 65 g fat (585 kcal) + 230 g carbs (920 kcal) = 2,125 kcal. Round up to 2,200 with a little flexibility in carbs.

**Why not a bigger deficit:** every 500 kcal/day you cut below this doubles the chance your Phase 2 hip thrust progression stalls. Glute growth is the priority, so the deficit stays small. **Why not maintenance:** you asked for visible abs, defined triceps, and a tighter waist. Those need body fat to come down, and a small deficit is the slowest, most muscle-sparing way to do it. **Why not a surplus:** you are not underweight and you have the body fat to recomp; eating more would add muscle slightly faster but push the leanness goal further away.

### Meal structure and protein distribution

4 meals, each with **35–45 g protein**, spaced 3–5 hours apart. Example on a Monday training day (training at 5 pm):

| Meal | Time | Protein | Carbs | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 8 am | 40 g | 50 g | Greek yogurt (1.5 cups) + berries + ½ cup oats + 1 tbsp nut butter |
| Lunch | 12:30 pm | 40 g | 60 g | 5 oz chicken or tofu, 1 cup rice, big salad with olive oil |
| **Pre-workout** | 3:30 pm | 25 g | 50 g | Banana + 1 scoop whey in milk, or toast + eggs |
| **Post-workout / dinner** | 6:30 pm | 45 g | 60 g | 6 oz lean beef, salmon, or shrimp, 1 cup potatoes/rice, vegetables |
| Optional evening | 9 pm | 15–20 g | — | Cottage cheese or casein shake if protein for the day is short |

Flex the carbs: push more of them toward the pre- and post-workout meals on Monday, Thursday, and Saturday; a little less on Sunday and Wednesday is fine, but keep protein and fat steady every day.

### Pre- and post-workout

- **Pre (1–2 hours before lifting):** 30–60 g carbs + 20–30 g protein, low fat and fiber so it digests. Caffeine 100–200 mg is fine if you tolerate it; avoid it after 2 pm if it affects sleep.
- **Post (within ~2 hours):** 30–45 g protein + 40–80 g carbs. There is no "anabolic window" that slams shut in 30 minutes; the meal matters, the stopwatch does not.
- **Heated Pilates and HIIT days:** eat a carb-containing snack 60–90 minutes before; never do heated classes fasted.
- **Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g daily,** is the one supplement with strong evidence for strength and lean mass. Optional, cheap, and safe for healthy adults; expect 1–3 lb of water weight gain in the first 1–2 weeks, which is not fat.

### Hydration

- Baseline: **2.5–3 L (85–100 oz) of fluid per day**, more on training days and much more on heated Pilates days (Section 7).
- Add **1–2 g of sodium per day** on top of a normal diet if you sweat a lot; salt your food, use an electrolyte mix on Wednesday and Saturday.
- Urine color should be pale yellow most of the day.

### How to adjust after 3 weeks of data

Use the **weekly average** body weight (Section 11), not any single day. Weight fluctuates 2–4 lb day to day from water, sodium, menstrual cycle, and carb intake.

| After weeks 2–4, if... | And lifts are... | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly average weight dropping 0.3–0.8 lb/week | Going up or stable | **Perfect. Change nothing.** |
| Weight dropping faster than 1% per week (>1.7 lb/week) for 2+ weeks | Stalling or dropping | Add 100–150 kcal/day (mostly carbs). You are in too deep a deficit. |
| Weight stable or up for 3 weeks | Going up strongly | Measure waist and hips. If waist is down and hips are stable or up, you are recomping; **change nothing**. If both are unchanged, cut 100–150 kcal/day. |
| Weight stable for 3 weeks | Stalling | Cut 100–150 kcal/day. Check protein and sleep first. |
| Energy crashing, sleep poor, always hungry, lifts falling | — | Return to maintenance (~2,400 kcal) for 1–2 weeks, then resume the deficit. |

Reassess at weeks 4, 8, and 12. Each adjustment is 100–150 kcal, never 500.

### How to tell you are losing fat while keeping or building muscle

You are winning if **two or more** of these are true over a 4-week block:
- Waist measurement down (even 0.5 inch) **while** hip/glute measurement holds or increases.
- Hip thrust and RDL loads or reps up.
- Progress photos show more shape in the glutes and more definition in the arms/midsection.
- Body weight is stable or down slowly.

If body weight is dropping fast *and* the hip measurement is dropping *and* lifts are down, you are losing muscle: eat more immediately.

### Not required

You do not need to reach a particular body weight to get the look you described. A 5'4" woman with well-developed glutes and visible abs can weigh anywhere from the 130s to the 160s depending on muscle mass and frame. Watch the measurements and photos, not the number on the scale.

## 10. Recovery, Hydration, and Sleep

- **Sleep: 7.5–9 hours.** This is the single biggest recovery variable. Below 7 hours for 3+ nights, muscle protein synthesis and training quality fall measurably. Keep a consistent wake time, dark cool room, no screens for 30 minutes before bed, and caffeine cutoff by early afternoon.
- **Rest day (Sunday) is not optional.** An easy walk is the most you should do. If you feel compelled to "do something," do 10 minutes of mobility: 90/90 hip switches, couch stretch, deep squat hold, thoracic rotations.
- **Soreness is not a progress metric.** You should be mildly sore after Monday and Thursday in weeks 1–3 and progressively less sore after that, even as weights go up. Severe soreness lasting 3+ days means the volume jumped too fast or sleep/food is short.
- **Sweat and exhaustion are not progress metrics either.** The best session is the one where you added a rep or 5 lb with good technique, not the one that left you destroyed.
- **Signs you are under-recovered:** resting heart rate up 5+ bpm for several days; waking tired after 8 hours; lifts going down at the same load two sessions in a row; irritability; unusual hunger or appetite loss; nagging joint aches; getting sick. Two or more means use the rules in Section 12.
- **Menstrual cycle:** expect 1–4 lb of water weight and possibly lower performance in the late luteal/early menstrual phase. Do not change calories because of it. Compare week to week at the same cycle phase if your weight pattern is cyclical.
- **Heat:** see Section 7. Heated classes count toward your total stress for the day; on Wednesdays, keep the evening quiet.

## 12. Rules for Modifying the Plan

### When you are sore

- **Mild soreness (moves fine, warms up out of it):** train as planned. Warm up an extra 5 minutes.
- **Moderate soreness in glutes/hamstrings on a heavy lower day:** do the session but hold loads at last week's numbers and stop at RIR 3. Skip the last set of each exercise.
- **Severe soreness (hard to sit, altered gait) on a lower day:** swap in Upper A or B if you haven't done it yet that week, or take an easy 30-minute walk. Resume lower-body work the next day.
- **Sore on Saturday morning:** keep Glute C, cut to 2 sets per exercise, replace HIIT with a 20-minute easy ride.
- Soreness that gets worse week over week means the volume or load increased too quickly. Drop one set per exercise for a week.

### When you are busy

Priority order if a week falls apart (do the top ones first):

1. Lower A (Monday hip thrust session)
2. Lower B (Thursday RDL session)
3. Upper A (Tuesday, triceps)
4. Upper B (Friday)
5. Glute C (Saturday pump)
6. Heated Pilates
7. Conditioning
8. Tuesday ride

Short-on-time versions:
- **30-minute Lower A:** hip thrust 3 sets, BSS 2 sets, reverse crunch 2 sets. Done.
- **30-minute Lower B:** RDL 3 sets, reverse lunge 2 sets, leg raise 2 sets.
- **25-minute Upper (either):** the first barbell press or row 3 sets, overhead extension or skull crusher 3 sets, close-grip push-ups 2 sets.
- Never try to make up a missed day by doubling the next day. Just continue the schedule. Two heavy lower days per week is the minimum that keeps progress; one week with only two is fine.

### When you are under-recovered

Two or more signs from Section 10 (poor sleep for several days, resting heart rate up, lifts down at the same load, irritability, joint aches):
- Cut the week to **2 sets per exercise at the same loads**, skip Saturday conditioning, keep Pilates gentle or skip it, keep walking.
- Eat at maintenance (~2,400 kcal) for that week.
- Sleep is the fix. Prioritize it over any single session.
- If it happens two weeks in a row, the deficit is too big or life stress is too high: hold calories at maintenance for 2 weeks and reduce HIIT to zero until you feel normal.

### When heated Pilates was harder than expected

See Section 7: rate the class. 6–7/10 → cut Saturday Glute C to 2 sets. 8+/10 → skip Glute C that week, keep Saturday conditioning easy, and pick a gentler class next time.

### When you hit an equipment ceiling

See Section 4.3: switch to the barbell variant first (free), then bands (cheap), then a heavier kettlebell or adjustable dumbbells.

### When you travel or lose the barbell

Hip thrust → single-leg hip thrust in vest, 4 × 12–15 each; RDL → single-leg KB/DB RDL and KB swings; squat → BSS and reverse lunges in vest; upper → push-ups, dips, kickbacks; keep protein at 150+ g. One week like this does not cost you progress.

## 13. Critical Missing Information

Answering these lets me tighten the loading and the Peloton selections. The plan as written works under the assumptions at the top; these questions make it *yours*.

1. **Training experience:** How many months or years have you lifted with a barbell? Have you followed a structured progressive program before, and for how long?
2. **Current working weights:** What can you currently hip thrust, RDL, and squat for 8–10 reps with 2–3 reps in reserve? If you have never done one of these, say so and week 1 becomes a pure learning week for that lift.

6. **Heated Pilates:** How many classes per week does your membership realistically allow, how hot are they, and are they reformer or mat? Which days are classes offered?
7. **Peloton hardware:** Bike only, or do you also have a Row or Tread? This changes the rowing and running options.
8. **Injuries or limitations:** Any current or past knee, hip, lower back, shoulder, or elbow issues? Any history of heat intolerance, fainting, or blood pressure concerns?
9. **Schedule constraints:** What time of day do you train, and are there days you cannot train at all? Are any of your Peloton programs on a fixed calendar you want to keep?
10. **Diet history:** Have you dieted before, and did you track calories? Any foods you don't eat (vegetarian, dairy-free, etc.)?

### Realistic 12-week expectations

What 12 weeks of this plan, done consistently, typically produces for someone in your position:
- Hip thrust and RDL loads up 40–100% from your week-1 working weights (more if you are a true beginner).
- Noticeably firmer, more "lifted" glutes; a measurable change in hip circumference of 0–1 inch (genetics and training age dependent). Significant glute *size* change is a 6–12 month project; this block is the foundation.
- Waist down 1–3 inches if nutrition is consistent, with the most visible change in weeks 8–12.
- Visible upper-ab and oblique definition depends entirely on your starting body fat; triceps definition usually shows earlier than abs.
- Body weight down 4–10 lb, **or** nearly unchanged with measurements improving. Both are success.

What I cannot promise is a specific look by a specific week. The indicators that matter are in the tracker: loads going up, waist going down, hips holding or growing, photos changing. If those move, the look follows.
